1. THE REAL-WORLD PROBLEM (What We Solved)
================================================================================
Imagine digging a deep underground tunnel to extract coal. Once millions of tons 
of coal are removed, huge empty rooms (voids) are left deep inside the earth.

Over weeks, months, or years, the heavy layers of rock and soil above these 
empty spaces start to bend, sag, and eventually collapse inward. This sinking 
of the earth's surface is called "MINE SUBSIDENCE".

Why is this a big problem?
- Roads, railway tracks, and power lines over the mine crack and break.
- Villages and houses built near the mine can develop huge cracks or cave in.
- Toxic gases or underground water can burst out.

The Old Method (Why it failed):
Currently, survey officers walk around once every few weeks with very expensive 
surveying tripods (DGPS/Total Stations). But if the ground starts sinking at 
2:00 AM on a rainy Tuesday night, nobody is there to see it until disaster strikes.

Our Mission:
Replace slow, expensive once-a-month human surveys with a smart, 24/7 automated 
early warning system that alerts engineers and villagers BEFORE a collapse happens!


================================================================================
2. OUR SOLUTION (How We Solved It)
================================================================================
We built "SurakshaMesh" — a dense, low-cost wireless network of smart sensor 
devices (called "Nodes") planted across the ground above the underground coal mine.

Think of it like a giant "digital safety net" spread over the surface:
1. Every node continuously feels the ground beneath it (measuring tiny tilts and shakes).
2. If any part of the ground begins to sink, the nearby nodes detect the tilt instantly.
3. The nodes talk to each other wirelessly using radio signals (like walkie-talkies).
4. An alarm sounds on the spot (Buzzer & Red Light), and an alert pops up on the 
   mine safety dashboard on laptops and mobile phones in real-time!

3. THE HARDWARE WE USED (What's Inside a Node?)
================================================================================
Each SurakshaMesh node in the first prototype is built from:

1. ESP32 Microcontroller (The "Brain"):
   - A tiny $4 computer chip that runs our software.
   - It has built-in Wi-Fi and a special fast radio protocol called "ESP-NOW".

2. MPU6050 Sensor (The "Inner Ear"):
   - Contains an Accelerometer (measures gravity and tilt) and a Gyroscope 
     (measures turning speed). 
   - This is the exact same chip inside your smartphone that knows when you rotate 
     the screen or count your steps.

3. RGB LED & Buzzer (The "Alarms"):
   - Acts like a traffic light right on the field:
     * GREEN  = Safe (Normal ground)
     * YELLOW = Watch (Minor ground movement detected)
     * RED    = Danger / Critical (Evacuate! Ground is collapsing!)
   - An active buzzer sounds a loud beeping tone during danger.

4. 3D-Printed Weatherproof Enclosure:
   - A strong, waterproof plastic shell designed with CAD code so rain, dust, 
     and coal dirt cannot damage the electronics.

4. WHAT IS THE FIRMWARE DOING? (The Code Inside the ESP32)
================================================================================
Firmware is the software running directly inside the chip. Here is what it does 
step-by-step, 50 times every single second:

Step 1: Read the Motion Sensor
- The chip reads raw data from the MPU6050 over two wires called I2C (SDA and SCL).
- It measures acceleration along 3 axes (X, Y, Z) and rotation speed.

Step 2: Smooth Out the Noise (The "Complementary Filter")
- In real life, wind or a passing truck causes tiny false vibrations. 
- The code uses a mathematical trick called a "Complementary Filter" (combining 
  98% smooth gyro tracking + 2% gravity checks) to find the true, steady tilt angle 
  (Pitch and Roll) without jitter.

Step 3: Measure Vibration RMS (Root-Mean-Square)
- The code keeps a rolling memory of the last 50 vibration readings to calculate 
  how violently the ground is shaking.

Step 4: The Smart Decision Brain (Risk Classification)
- The code checks simple safety rules:
  * Tilt < 2°: Risk = 0 (GREEN / Normal)
  * Tilt between 2° and 5°: Risk = 1 (YELLOW / Watch)
  * Tilt > 8° OR sustained vibration: Risk = 3 (RED / Critical Alert!)

Step 5: Broadcast Over the Air (ESP-NOW Radio)
- Every second, the node packs its ID, Pitch, Roll, Vibration, and Risk into a tiny 
  digital packet and transmits it through the air directly to the Gateway node.

5. THE BACKEND & DASHBOARD (How the Screen Works)
================================================================================
1. Central Gateway Node (NODE-01):
   - One ESP32 stays plugged into a laptop via USB cable. It listens to all the 
     field nodes over radio, receives their packets, and sends them out as 
     JSON text lines over the USB Serial Port (115200 baud).

2. Serial Bridge (Node.js / Python):
   - A background script reads the serial USB port and forwards the live telemetry 
     data to the web application over fast WebSockets and HTTP APIs.

3. Real-Time Web Dashboard (Next.js 16, React 19 & Tailwind CSS):
   - Shows a beautiful interactive 3D map of hexagonal cells representing the mine.
   - When you physically tilt a node on your desk or wooden plank, the matching 
     hexagon on the screen turns from glowing green to bright red in under 50 milliseconds!
   - Features sound alerts, 3D tilt models, and live CSV data logging.

6. WHERE DOES THE "AI" COME IN? (Artificial Intelligence Explained)
================================================================================
1. Isolation Forest Machine Learning:
   - Learns the quiet baseline of the mine surface.
   - Flags anomalous deformation trends before visible cracks appear.

2. Blast vs. Subsidence Classifier:
   - Dynamite blasting causes a huge shake, but tilt quickly returns to normal.
   - Real subsidence causes a shake AND leaves permanent ground tilt.
   - Our system avoids false alarms during mine blast hours.

3. Spatial Coherence (Mesh Teamwork):
   - 1 node shaking = local noise / vehicle.
   - 3 adjacent nodes tilting together = confirmed large-scale strata subsidence!

8. THE NEXT PHASE FOR SIH FINALE: UPGRADING TO LoRa & MASSIVE SCALING
================================================================================
Why switch from Wi-Fi/ESP-NOW to LoRa?
- ESP-NOW / Wi-Fi is great for benchtop prototypes and 50–100 meter demos.
- In a real open-cast or underground coal mine (spanning 2 km to 5 km), 2.4 GHz 
  Wi-Fi signals cannot penetrate through rocky hills and coal spoil dumps.

How LoRa (Long Range) Solves This:
- LoRa operates on sub-GHz frequencies (868 MHz in India / Europe or 433 MHz).
- Range: 2 km to 10+ km with tiny antennas, penetrating through dust, rain, and rocks!
- Power: Uses 10x less power than Wi-Fi.

Hardware Needed for Finale:
- SX1278 (Ra-02) or SX1262 LoRa SPI modules connected to each ESP32.
  * SCK  -> GPIO 18
  * MISO -> GPIO 19
  * MOSI -> GPIO 23
  * NSS  -> GPIO 5
  * RST  -> GPIO 14
  * DIO0 -> GPIO 2

How to Control 50 to 100+ Nodes:
1. LoRaWAN / Slotted TDMA Mesh:
   - Each node is assigned a unique Node ID (e.g., NODE-01 to NODE-99).
   - Nodes send a short 16-byte packet every 1 to 5 minutes (or immediately on alert).
2. Multi-Hop Mesh Routing:
   - Faraway nodes transmit packets to intermediate nodes, which "hop" the data 
     forward until it reaches the central mine office gateway.

9. POWER MANAGEMENT: SOLAR + 18650 BATTERY (Years of Autonomous Life)
================================================================================
In a real mine, you cannot plug sensors into wall sockets or swap power banks daily.

The Solar Power Architecture:
1. 18650 Lithium-Ion Battery (3.7V, 2600 mAh - protected cell).
2. TP4056 Solar Battery Charger Module with over-discharge protection (DW01).
3. 1W–2W 6V Monocrystalline Mini Solar Panel mounted on the top lid of the enclosure.
4. Ultra-Low Power Deep Sleep:
   - The ESP32 stays in deep sleep consuming only 10 micro-amperes (0.01 mA).
   - Every 5 minutes, it wakes up, reads the sensor for 200 ms, sends a LoRa packet, 
     and goes back to sleep.
   - Hardware Interrupt: If the MPU6050 detects sudden violent shaking or tilt, 
     its INT pin instantly wakes up the ESP32 to send an emergency alert immediately!
   - Result: The node can run for 3 to 5+ YEARS with zero maintenance!

10. SENSOR EXPANSIONS: CRACK & MULTI-LAYER GEOTECHNICAL SENSORS
================================================================================
To make SurakshaMesh the ultimate geotechnical safety system for Coal India:

1. Surface Tension Crack Sensors (Flex / Linear Potentiometer):
   - As subsidence develops, the ground stretches before dropping. 
   - A flex sensor or spring-loaded draw-wire across known fault lines measures 
     millimeter-level crack opening.

2. Draw-Wire Extensometer Between Adjacent Nodes:
   - A thin stainless steel cable connecting two posts. If the distance between 
     the posts increases or decreases by even 2 mm, it measures lateral ground strain.

3. Cellular 4G / GSM Fallback (Central Gateway):
   - The central gateway uses a SIM800L / SIM7600 4G LTE module to send instant 
     emergency SMS alerts directly to mine managers, DGMS officials, and nearby villagers.

11. ADVANCED AI ROADMAP: LSTM & KNOTHE SUBSIDENCE PREDICTION CURVE
================================================================================
For the Grand Finale presentation:

1. Knothe Time-Factor Model (Mining Geology Math):
   - Predicts the exact 3D subsidence bowl (trough) forming above the coal seam based 
     on panel extraction speed and mining depth.

2. Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Neural Network:
   - Trained on weeks of time-series tilt logs.
   - Forecasts: "Based on the last 48 hours of slope creep, this zone will reach 
     critical danger threshold (8°) in approximately 14 hours."
   - Gives mine rescue teams valuable time to evacuate heavy machinery and personnel!
